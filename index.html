Why this platform exists

HIV, ART and resistance—briefly explained

HIV attacks immune cells. Antiretroviral therapy (ART) uses combinations of medicines to suppress the virus. When viral mutations reduce a medicine’s effectiveness, resistance information can help researchers understand which drug classes may be affected. This prototype organizes mutation records, therapy signals, interaction references and evidence in one workspace.

Educational research prototype
HIV particles viewed by transmission electron microscopy. Credit: CDC / A. Harrison, P. Feorino and E. L. Palmer. Public domain. CDC PHIL 14272.

What is HIV?

A virus that targets the immune system, especially CD4 cells, and can progress without effective treatment.

What is ART?

Antiretroviral therapy combines medicines from one or more classes to prevent HIV from replicating effectively.

Why track mutations?

Mutation patterns can indicate reduced susceptibility and help structure further resistance review and clinical interpretation.

Why combination therapy matters

ART combines medicines that interrupt different stages of HIV replication. Resistance-associated mutations may reduce susceptibility to one or more drugs, which is why this page groups local records by actionability rather than displaying a single undifferentiated list.

  • Avoid highlights records with the strongest local warning.
  • Use With Caution identifies intermediate or context-dependent records.
  • Potential Options means no matching mutation from that class was detected here; it is not a prescribing recommendation.

What the assistant can and cannot do

Dataset-grounded explanations

The assistant answers from the local mutation records and current analysis. It can explain mutation codes, summarize detected records and describe prototype recommendations.

  • It is rules-based rather than a validated clinical AI system.
  • It does not replace expert genotype interpretation.
  • Questions are most useful when they include a mutation code or refer to the current analysis.
